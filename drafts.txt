Here's a version that is professional, constructive, and focused on business impact rather than frustration.

Hi Team,

First, thank you for confirming that we'll be getting access to the Cline CLI. This is a great step forward, and I appreciate the effort the AI Infrastructure team is putting into enabling AI adoption within Samsung.

I wanted to share some feedback and a request from the perspective of the OSI/IMS troubleshooting workflow.

While coding assistance is certainly valuable, our team's primary productivity bottleneck is not code generation—it's **bug analysis and troubleshooting**. Every day we deal with large customer issues involving extensive log collection, protocol traces, and multiple artifacts. Today, much of this process is manual: downloading logs, opening multiple tools, searching through traces, correlating events, and iterating repeatedly. This is time-consuming and directly impacts how quickly we can resolve customer issues and achieve project milestones.

Recent LLMs have demonstrated exceptional capabilities in this area. We've already seen that, with the right prompts and tools, they can quickly identify protocol issues, correlate logs, explain failures, and significantly accelerate root cause analysis.

For teams like ours, AI-assisted troubleshooting has the potential to deliver even greater productivity gains than AI-assisted coding.

### 1. Prioritize CLI access for troubleshooting teams

I would request that access be prioritized based on expected business impact rather than only coding use cases. Although our team may not be among the heaviest users of AI for code generation, we expect to derive substantial value from AI-driven troubleshooting.

### 2. Consider providing an SDK or API for agentic applications

The CLI is a very good first step, but it primarily enables individual workflows.

Many of the efficiency gains we are seeing across the industry now come from **agentic AI**—applications that orchestrate multiple steps automatically, integrate with internal tools, process large collections of logs, maintain workflows, and automate repetitive troubleshooting tasks.

Building these kinds of applications is much easier with an SDK or API than with a CLI alone. Even if direct SDK access isn't immediately possible, it would be helpful to understand whether exposing an API or another supported integration mechanism is on the roadmap.

Our vision is to build internal troubleshooting assistants that can:

* Collect and organize customer logs
* Analyze multiple artifacts together
* Correlate protocol events automatically
* Produce root cause hypotheses
* Suggest next debugging steps
* Integrate into our existing engineering workflow

This would allow engineers to spend more time solving problems and less time manually navigating tools and logs.

I believe investing in agentic AI infrastructure will significantly improve engineering productivity across many domains beyond coding, particularly in debugging, validation, testing, and customer issue resolution.

Thanks again for enabling the CLI, and I hope we can continue expanding the platform to support these broader engineering use cases.

---
